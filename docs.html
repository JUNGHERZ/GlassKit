Theming (Light / Dark)

The theme is controlled via data-theme on the <html> element.

// Theme per JavaScript wechseln: function toggleTheme() { const html = document.documentElement; const current = html.getAttribute('data-theme'); html.setAttribute('data-theme', current === 'dark' ? 'light' : 'dark' ); }

Primary as text

The primary does two jobs. As a fill it carries white text: buttons, the checked toggle, the current step, the chosen day. As text it stands on the page or the glass itself: links in .glass-prose, the icon and label of the active tab, the primary modal action, accent list rows, the value of a range, the accordion trigger under the pointer and the chevron of an open section. Since 1.26.0 the second job has a token of its own, --gl-color-primary-text. Unset by a project, it is the primary, so nothing changes.

A brand that darkens its primary so that white button text passes 4.5:1 gets a tone too dark for text on a dark background. Lighten the text in the dark theme, and the buttons keep their colour:

:root, [data-theme="dark"] { --gl-color-primary-text: color-mix(in srgb, var(--gl-color-primary) 18%, #ffffff); } [data-theme="light"] { --gl-color-primary-text: var(--gl-color-primary); }

Write the light rule after the dark one: :root matches a page in the light theme too, and of two matching rules the later one wins. The right tone depends on the background, so measure it there. On GlassKit’s own dark background with #c25028 as primary, this mix takes a link on a glass card from 1.37:1 to 5.05:1.

The active tab takes --gl-tab-bar-active-color for its icon and label where that is set, else --gl-color-primary-text. The spotlight of the floating bar lightens the active item more than any other surface, so a brand may need a lighter tone there, up to white. Set it per theme like the text colour; var(--gl-color-primary-text) in the light rule gives the default back. Glow and dot stay in the primary, the spotlight in --gl-tab-bar-spotlight-color.

Since 1.31.0 muted text, card text and icons reach WCAG AA by default, in both themes. The primary as text does not, on purpose: GlassKit’s own orange reads 2.43:1 as text on the light page, but a darker brand may pass already or need far less, and a fixed mix with black would darken every brand alike. Where the light theme keeps the orange, darken it there; --gl-color-primary-on-surface, the ink of the primary badge, reaches 5.8:1. White on the primary button is part of the look as well, 2.7:1 in the light theme and 2.0:1 in the dark one on the lightest stop of the gradient; --gl-color-on-primary switches it to a dark ink that reaches 4.6:1 or more on every stop:

[data-theme="light"] { --gl-color-primary-text: var(--gl-color-primary-on-surface); } /* Dark text on the primary button, in both themes */ :root, [data-theme="dark"], [data-theme="light"] { --gl-color-on-primary: color-mix(in srgb, var(--gl-color-primary) 17%, #000); }

Since 1.32.0 both ways have tokens of their own. To keep white text, darken the fill and leave the brand colour alone, which also draws focus rings, borders, the tab bar’s spotlight and the accent as text. --gl-btn-primary-bg is the whole background of the primary button, unset today’s gradient; --gl-color-primary-fill is the flat accent fill that carries text — the chosen day of date strip and calendar, the number of the current step —, unset the primary. On GlassKit’s own orange, white reaches 4.5:1 from 75 % primary in the light theme and from 65 % in the dark one, and the gradient below stays at or above that on every point:

/* White text on a darker fill: the button, the chosen days, the current step */ :root, [data-theme="dark"] { --gl-color-primary-fill: color-mix(in srgb, var(--gl-color-primary) 65%, #000); } [data-theme="light"] { --gl-color-primary-fill: color-mix(in srgb, var(--gl-color-primary) 75%, #000); } :root, [data-theme="dark"], [data-theme="light"] { --gl-btn-primary-bg: linear-gradient(135deg, var(--gl-color-primary-fill), color-mix(in srgb, var(--gl-color-primary-dark) 80%, #000)); }

On the chosen day of the date strip the weekday is now as strong as the number (since 1.32.0); faded, it stayed below 4.5:1 even on a fill the number reaches it on.

The dark ink reaches the radio now as well: its dot follows --gl-color-on-primary unless --gl-radio-dot-color is set, as the checkbox tick does (since 1.32.0). Where the brand colour is set at run time, the browser can pick the ink: contrast-color() gives black or white, whichever stands out more against the primary — black for GlassKit’s orange in both themes, 5.5:1 or more on every stop of the button. The 17 % ink stays the fallback where a browser does not know it yet:

:root, [data-theme="dark"], [data-theme="light"] { --gl-color-on-primary: color-mix(in srgb, var(--gl-color-primary) 17%, #000); } @supports (color: contrast-color(red)) { :root, [data-theme="dark"], [data-theme="light"] { --gl-color-on-primary: contrast-color(var(--gl-color-primary)); } }

contrast-color() looks at the primary alone. With a fill of your own, or a gradient that ends much darker, measure the other end as well.

Right to left

Since 1.20.0 the components follow the writing direction. Set dir="rtl" on <html> or on any container: paddings, insets and borders are logical properties, so labels and hints, the search icon, list dividers, popover anchors, tab badges, modal actions, the toast’s close button and the alignment of accordion, list, table and prose all mirror. Where two values have to move together — the select’s chevron and the room kept for it, the toggle’s thumb and its travel, the progress fill’s gradient — the rule sits under :dir(rtl): Chrome and Edge from 120, Safari from 16.4, Firefox from 49. An older browser keeps those parts left to right instead of tearing them apart.

Printing

Print dialogs leave out background graphics by default, and some states are drawn with a background only. Since 1.21.1 those parts keep their colours on paper — the checked box and its tick, the chosen radio, the toggle’s track, the progress fill, the current step, the chosen day of date strip and calendar, the slider thumb and the tone dots — while the rest of the page prints economically. Nothing to set. A dark page prints its light text and surfaces on white paper; a page meant for printing switches to the light theme for it:

let printTheme = null; addEventListener('beforeprint', () => { printTheme = document.documentElement.getAttribute('data-theme'); document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-theme', 'light'); }); addEventListener('afterprint', () => { if (printTheme) document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-theme', printTheme); else document.documentElement.removeAttribute('data-theme'); });

Density (Compact)

Admin screens, desktop layouts and narrow forms want denser controls. Since 1.21.0 the control sizes are density tokens, and data-density="compact" on the <html> element swaps in a smaller set: fields and buttons 40px high instead of 52 and 56, smaller toggles, checkboxes and radios, denser list rows and modal actions. Colours, type and spacing stay as they are. Without the attribute every control keeps the size it has always had.

Default
Compact

The preview sets the attribute on its right column. On a page it belongs on <html>, next to data-theme: an element further down that carries data-theme="dark" re-declares the default sizes.

<html data-theme="dark" data-density="compact"> // Switch at runtime: document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-density', 'compact'); document.documentElement.removeAttribute('data-density'); /* One token for both densities – or for compact only: */ :root { --gl-btn-height: 48px; } [data-density='compact'] { --gl-btn-height: 36px; }

The toggle's thumb inset and travel — in either writing direction — and the first-line alignment of toggle, checkbox and radio are derived from the tokens, so any size a project sets stays aligned. The rules read every size with today's value as fallback, like --gl-modal-max-width, so a page whose token sheet predates them keeps the old sizes.

Surfaces (Solid, Tint)

Not every screen wants glass: admin screens, forms on a plain background, designs without it. Since 1.24.0 data-surface="solid" on the <html> element makes every surface opaque and turns blur and gloss off. Each surface takes the colour the glass shows over the theme’s middle background, --gl-color-bg-mid: cards, fields and buttons keep their order, and a re-branded background carries over. The inner shine (--gl-inset-*), the highlight along the top edge of the glow card and the modal, the sheen on the progress fill and the rim of the range thumb are off. Borders, drop shadows, the tints of badges and chips and the dimming overlay stay as they are. Without the attribute nothing changes.

A page with a background of its own — a brand colour, a photo, a pattern — takes data-surface="tint" instead (since 1.25.0). A surface mixed over --gl-color-bg-mid would stand out there as a foreign block, so the surfaces in the flow of the page become see-through tints of the text colour, nothing blurred: a field on a card stays as see-through as the card, and its contrast follows the text. What lies over other content keeps the solid values — modal, sheet, popover, toast and the tab bars, with the fields and rows inside them — and so does the secondary button, a light plate for dark ink. Blur and gloss are off as with solid; a blur behind dialogs stays where a project sets --gl-blur-overlay.

Icons and tokens

Since 1.28.0 every state colours the icon box, and an SVG strokes in currentColor. So the icon can be anything that takes a colour — a font icon such as Material Icons, an <img> drawn as a mask, an element of your own — and it follows active and hover, the lens of the drop and the drag. A font icon takes the box’s 24 px unless its own class sets a size. Give it aria-hidden="true": its text would join the tab’s name. Sidebar, rail and top bar

Since 1.32.0 the same navigation goes where the layout needs it, not only along the bottom edge. The items stay as they are — icon, label, badge, the active state, the drop — and only the bar’s layout changes. --vertical makes it a column for a sidebar: placed by the page’s layout, a grid column for example, and as high as its container, one item per row with the icon before the label at body size and the badge at the row’s end. The active row stands on a surface of its own, --gl-tab-bar-active-bg where that is set, else --gl-surface-3. With --rail as well, the column shows the icons alone, --gl-tab-bar-rail-width (72 px) wide: the label stays the item’s name for assistive technology, hidden from view, so show it in a tooltip.

<div class="app"> <!-- display: grid; grid-template-columns: 220px 1fr; height: 100dvh --> <nav class="glass-tab-bar glass-tab-bar--vertical" aria-label="Sections"> <a class="glass-tab-bar__item is-active" href="/"> <span class="glass-tab-bar__icon"><!-- SVG --></span> <span class="glass-tab-bar__label">Home</span> </a> <a class="glass-tab-bar__item" href="/invoices"> <span class="glass-tab-bar__icon"><!-- SVG --><span class="glass-tab-bar__badge">3</span></span> <span class="glass-tab-bar__label">Invoices</span> </a> </nav> <main>…</main> </div> <!-- Icons alone --> <nav class="glass-tab-bar glass-tab-bar--vertical glass-tab-bar--rail" aria-label="Sections">…</nav>

With --start or --end the column is fixed to that edge of the viewport instead, full height and clear of that edge’s safe area; start and end follow the writing direction. That is the side the thumbs reach on a large phone held sideways or a foldable held open, where the system moves its own controls to the edge as well. The page keeps that side free, as it keeps the bottom free for the bar there. The items stay when the layout changes, so the page switches the classes — on a media query, or by the user’s choice:

<nav class="glass-tab-bar" id="tabs" aria-label="Sections">…</nav> <script> const tabs = document.getElementById('tabs'); const side = matchMedia('(orientation: landscape) and (min-width: 700px)'); const layout = () => { tabs.classList.toggle('glass-tab-bar--vertical', side.matches); tabs.classList.toggle('glass-tab-bar--start', side.matches); // the page keeps the bar's side free document.body.style.paddingInlineStart = side.matches ? `${tabs.offsetWidth}px` : ''; }; side.addEventListener('change', layout); layout(); </script>

--top lays a horizontal bar out for the top of a page: in the flow — put it in a sticky header —, its border at the bottom, no room for the home indicator, and from a viewport of 640 px the icon beside the label.

<header style="position: sticky; top: 0; z-index: 10"> <nav class="glass-tab-bar glass-tab-bar--top" aria-label="Sections">…</nav> </header>

Every bar shows a ring for the keyboard now, --gl-shadow-focus on the focused item; until 1.32.0 the items had none. An item may also be a link, <a class="glass-tab-bar__item" href="…">, so a view opens in a new tab as well; it looks like the button. In GlassKit Elements the layouts are attributes: <glk-tab-bar orientation="vertical"> with rail (or rail="labels") and dock="start" or "end", and placement="top"; slot="leading" and "trailing" fill the places before and after the items.

A compact top bar

Three tokens since 1.33.0 shape the items without a change to GlassKit’s global ones: --gl-tab-bar-icon-size sizes the icons, --gl-tab-bar-item-radius rounds the drop and the floating items — unset a capsule —, and --gl-tab-bar-active-weight sets the active label’s weight. A rail with labels

Since 1.33.0 --labels next to --rail keeps the label under the icon, as on the floating bar: a desktop rail that names its items. The items fill the rail’s width, --gl-tab-bar-rail-width, and are at least --gl-tab-bar-rail-item-height (62 px) high; --gl-tab-bar-item-radius rounds them and the drop, unset 16 px, the corners of a button. A long label ends in an ellipsis, and the badge stays at the icon’s corner.

__leading and __trailing are places inside the bar before and after the items: in a column above them — a mark, the accessory — and at its far end — a foot such as the account; in a row before and after them. .glass-tab-bar__accessory-item stands the accessory over its name, __accessory-label. The name is the button’s aria-label as well, so the visible one is aria-hidden, and a click on it should press the button.

With --floating the rail is a pane of its own glass, docked or not: as high as its container, with the corners of a card, --gl-tab-bar-rail-radius, and room around its items — in the flow, a grid column of the page, as in the preview. With --start or --end as well, it stands at that edge of the viewport instead: as high as the viewport less --gl-tab-bar-floating-inset (16 px) at both ends, that far from its edge, clear of the safe areas and mirrored right to left. The compact upright pill of the vertical dock, without --rail, stays a capsule.

Tab-Bar – Floating

Pill-shaped, centered, floating variant inspired by iOS 26 Liquid Glass. Pair with an optional .glass-tab-bar__accessory capsule (e.g. search, compose) — sits next to the bar with its own glass material. Active item surface

Since 1.27.0 the active item can stand on a surface of its own, like the selected tab of iOS 26. Four tokens, all unset, so nothing changes until a project sets them: --gl-tab-bar-active-bg and --gl-tab-bar-active-shadow draw a capsule behind the active item, --gl-tab-bar-spotlight-opacity: 0 turns the floating bar’s spotlight off, and --gl-tab-bar-active-glow: none the glow on the icon, which reads as a blur on a light capsule.

:root { --gl-tab-bar-active-bg: color-mix(in srgb, var(--gl-color-text) 14%, transparent); --gl-tab-bar-active-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.12), var(--gl-inset-subtle); --gl-tab-bar-spotlight-opacity: 0; --gl-tab-bar-active-glow: none; }

A nearly opaque white capsule, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.9), lets a dark brand primary stay the active tab’s colour in the dark theme, where the spotlight would leave it unreadable. Droplet

Since 1.27.0 .glass-tab-bar--droplet makes the selection fluid. The active item sits on a drop of glass that floats a little above the bar. Tap another tab and the drop runs there like water on a pane: the leading edge goes first, the trailing one follows, the drop runs flatter on the way, settles with a small wobble, and the icons it passes grow as under a lens. A finger can also drag it along the bar; let go, and the tab below it is chosen. It is the most noticeable upgrade an app on the floating bar can take, and it changes nothing else: the markup stays, and where the script does not run the bar keeps its spotlight. Try it:

The stylesheet draws the drop. The motion comes from glasskit-droplet.js, an optional module in the same package without dependencies, about 4 KB minified and under 2 KB compressed. It follows .is-active, which the page keeps setting its own way, and clicks the tab a drag ends on, so the page’s own click handler makes the choice:

<nav class="glass-tab-bar glass-tab-bar--floating glass-tab-bar--droplet"> <!-- the items, the active one with .is-active --> </nav> <script type="module"> import { attachDroplets } from 'https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@jungherz-de/glasskit@1.33/glasskit-droplet.js'; attachDroplets(); // every --droplet row in the page; attachDroplet(row) for one </script>

The drop takes the active item’s surface where a project set one (--gl-tab-bar-active-bg, -shadow), else a light glass of its own; its highlight is --gl-gloss-droplet. data-surface="solid" and "tint" make it opaque like every other surface, and with prefers-reduced-motion it moves without travelling. A transform on an ancestor — an app that zooms in on start, a popover that opens from scale(0.96) — leaves it on its item, at rest and while the transform runs (since 1.28.0). Segmented controls take the same drop. In GlassKit Elements it is one attribute: <glk-tab-bar floating droplet>.

In a column — a sidebar, the upright floating bar — the drop runs along y (since 1.32.0). The script reads the row’s flex-direction or its aria-orientation="vertical", sets data-droplet-axis="y" and the directions "down" and "up", and a finger drags the drop up and down while a sideways swipe goes to the page. Wrapping text

Title and subtitle keep to one line, cut with an ellipsis, and --wrap clamps the subtitle at three lines. In the top layer

Since 1.32.0 a popover can open in the top layer. Segmented

A small, exclusive choice as one control — traffic light, morning / afternoon, mode. Buttons inside a role="group"; the chosen one carries aria-pressed="true", and the styling hangs on exactly that attribute, so state and semantics cannot drift apart. Icons

Since 1.28.0 an option can show an icon in .glass-segmented__icon, before its label: an SVG strokes in the option’s colour, any other element — a font icon, an <img> mask — takes that colour, and a font icon the box’s 18 px. Under the drop the icon grows like the tone dot. --icons makes a row of icons alone, a light / dark / auto switch for instance: the options become square, and each takes its name as aria-label, and as title for a tooltip. Counts

Since 1.29.0 an option can say how much waits behind it — open invoices, new tickets — with .glass-segmented__badge after its label: a small pill in tabular figures, inline, so a scrolling row and the drop measure the option with it. The tone dot says that something is there, the badge how much. Without a tone it is neutral, a tint of the text colour over the state scrim; with one it takes the state tint of the badges. Put inside .glass-segmented__icon it sits at the icon’s corner, as on the tab bar — the place for an --icons row, where it covers the icon and is opaque. Its number is no name: give the option an aria-label that says it, “Tickets, 1 open”.

<button class="glass-segmented__item glass-segmented__item--warning" aria-pressed="false" aria-label="Tickets, 1 open"> <span class="glass-segmented__dot"></span>Tickets<span class="glass-segmented__badge">1</span> </button> <!-- In an --icons row: at the icon's corner --> <span class="glass-segmented__icon" aria-hidden="true"><!-- SVG --><span class="glass-segmented__badge">3</span></span>

The neutral pill has two tokens, both colours and unset by default: --gl-segmented-badge-bg, the tint over the scrim (unset: 12 % of the text colour), and --gl-segmented-badge-color (unset: the text colour). Measured in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox, the figures reach 5.6:1 or more in the dark theme and 4.7:1 or more in the light one, on the chosen option and beside it, neutral and in every tone.

Since 1.31.0 a count can carry a colour of its own, whatever the option’s tone: .glass-segmented__badge--primary, --success, --warning and --error give it the look of the badge variants — the state tint over a doubled scrim, a hairline, the readable text colour — without the dot a tone puts before the label. Where the option has a tone as well, the count’s own colour wins. Its size is a token too: --gl-segmented-badge-height (unset 16 px, also its least width) and --gl-segmented-badge-font-size (unset 11 px). --gl-segmented-badge-border draws a hairline in that colour around the neutral count, inside, so the count keeps its size. Measured in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox, the four colours reach 4.7:1 or more in both themes, inline and at the icon’s corner, on the chosen option and beside it.

Droplet
